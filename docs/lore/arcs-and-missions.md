# Arcs and missions: a planning draft

Drafted 2026-11-01 by Claude Opus 5.5 for Kyle and the lore keeper, from `series-research.md`,
`story-bible.md`, `voice.md`, `research-notes.md` section 6 and the code as it stands (`story.ts`,
`jobs.ts`, `terrain.ts`, `works.ts`). Nothing here is canon yet. **[NEW]** marks anything the bible
doesn't already hold, which needs the lore keeper's yes. Quoted job postings are drafts and still go
through the lore keeper and the line editor.

## 1. What the books teach for this kind of story

1. **Every job takes something, even a won one.** Each big set piece in the first book ends with a
   loss. A job's end screen should say what it cost as plainly as what it saved. (series-research 2a)
2. **Sound and approach first, the fight second.** The books' worst dread is something arriving where
   there's no way out. Open a run on a noise and shut a door behind the party before anything swings.
   (2a)
3. **Captivity is the scariest place.** The scenes readers call nightmares put the heroes inside the
   enemy's routine, watching it work. (1a, 3b)
4. **Horror lives in a custom.** The death readers remember most is legal, pointless, and watched by
   people who go back to their day. Relic's worst moment should come from one of its own rules. (3b)
5. **What you carry hunts you.** Twice the series puts its threat inside the party. Relic already has
   the pieces: hungry gear, a full censer, relics. (2c, 2d)
6. **Hold the real goal back, then stack the reveals.** The first book's true mission lands well past
   halfway, once readers care about the people; its big reveals all come at the end. Relic's stack
   belongs at a vote. (2a)
7. **The worst blow comes from your own side.** In every book it's a guild, a king or a family. Each
   chapter wants a human hand behind the hungry. (2d)
8. **The winners pay for the win.** The second book's victory is followed by the victors turning on
   whoever won it, and it ends on a small private grief, not a parade. (1b)
9. **Episodes are loved only when they move the thread.** All three books are faulted for drift. Every
   job should push one bar on the chapter's goal. (3c)
10. **When the jokes go, warmth carries the weight.** The darkest book's loudest complaint is missing
    humour; readers held on through friendships and animals. (2b, 3c)

## 2. The long arc

All four arcs end where the bible's spine ends, at the Hungry King and the bells; they differ in the
road there, and they can be blended (a suggestion closes the section). Each starts from chapter 1's
vote (`north-fields`: farm or refugees).

**Secrets every arc keeps** (from the bible): the Lantern being a shard surfaces only in the last
vote's stack, if at all; the Unmaking is unknown until the keep's vote; the Hungry King is a rumour
before he's a face; the Tally Man never appears before his chapter; the Want is never named on screen
(the eyes are its only tell), nor why the hungry come for a Healer.

### Arc A: The Spine, sharpened

**Pitch.** The bible's four chapters in the bible's order, with the books' lessons fitted in: a human
hand behind each threat, a pointless loss in the middle, a costly one at the end, and a short coda
where the town treats its heroes badly. Least new lore, and half seeded already (the sewers work, the
Old Quarter work, the tally wall rumour).

- **Ch 2, What Lies Under.** Sewer jobs, Bring Down the Workings. Reveal: the tunnels all point at the
  square. A short beat mid-chapter: one of the watch on the wall in boots the town didn't pay for.
  Vote (bible): seal the sewers, or go down after the diggers.
- **Ch 3, The Barred Keep.** The Lane at Night once the Old Quarter is clear. The pointless loss
  **[NEW]**: someone the town likes is outside when the north gate shuts at dark, and the watch keeps
  the gate law the siege taught them while the town listens from the walls, then goes in to supper.
  Farmer Jos if the town farmed, the refugee trader if it opened the gate. Vote (bible): kill Lord
  Wick and take the heirloom, or open the chapel.
- **Ch 4, The Tally Market.** The Terms. Reveal: the ledger has a Wickhold name on the buyer's side,
  whoever in the watch took the bribe. Vote (bible): trade, or burn.
- **Ch 5, The Hungry King.** Hold Till Morning, Carry It Home. The stack lands at the vote: what he
  wants, why the Lantern, who called the hungry in at Barrowby. Vote (bible): carry the Lantern out,
  or ring every bell and hold.
- **Ch 6, Morning (coda) [NEW].** Short. The council counts the cost and somebody must be blamed for
  it; it ends small and private, with a dog.

**Reveals:** ch 2 the tunnels; ch 3 what's left of Wick (and, if the chapel opens, that the church kept
a cure behind nails); ch 4 the traitor's price; ch 5 the stack.
**Cost:** the sewers or some of its names, a liked townsperson, Lord Wick one way or another, the
market's prisoners or the crown piece, then the Lantern or a hero.
**Votes:** north-fields, the four bible votes, and a coda vote **[NEW]** on whom the town blames (the
heroes, the watch, or the church).

### Arc B: The Ledger

**Pitch.** The road is about the count: who is still out there, where they were taken, and what each
one costs to bring back. It runs on the books' most frightening material (captivity, the enemy's
paperwork) and the bible's sharpest fact, that the pens are where the hungry are made. The rumour of
the Fennick Ford wall with more marks than people is its first page.

- **Ch 2, The Wall [NEW].** Read the Wall jobs and the Count. Reveal: some marks are newer than the
  siege, people taken from inside Wickhold in eleven years of supposed peace. Vote: chalk the count up
  in the square (grief, anger, more volunteers), or give it to Odric only (quiet, and one more church
  secret).
- **Ch 3, What Lies Under.** The tunnels are the road the missing were taken by. Bible vote.
- **Ch 4, The Pens [NEW].** Captives come home who've been penned since harvest, some with eyes Sister
  Agne doesn't like. The pointless loss: someone brought home turns, on Rescue Row, among the bread.
  Vote: Rescue Row keeps everyone it's given, or the church takes the long-kept ones and doesn't say
  where.
- **Ch 5, The Tally Market.** He holds the only full record of where everyone went. Bible vote, now
  weighed down by two chapters of names.
- **Ch 6, The Hungry King.** Bible vote.

**Reveals:** ch 2 the inside names; ch 4 that the pens make the hungry (shown, never said); ch 5 the
ledger's last page **[NEW]**, the eleven Hew burned booked as stock lost, beside the order that sent
the hungry north.
**Cost:** Rescue Row's peace once it learns what it took in, trust in the church, the ledger itself if
the market burns.
**Votes:** north-fields (refugees means a fuller Rescue Row, more at risk), wall, sewers, pens, market,
bells.

### Arc C: The Bell [NEW]

**Pitch.** Wickhold melted its bells for arrowheads and has been ashamed of it for eleven years. After
Barrowby it decides to cast one, and a bell can only be cast from metal people give up: pots,
buckles, rings. That puts the bible's two big truths in one object (a town agreeing; giving keeps you
human) and gives chapter 5's bells option something real to ring. Most new lore, built on the one
rule the bible says "will matter".

- **Ch 2, The Founder.** The one person left who knows how to cast a bell is penned at Lowfurrow.
  Bring them home (the Count). They want a casting pit in the square, and the digging breaks into a
  tunnel pointed at the Lantern: chapter 2's reveal, arriving through the town's own hope. Bible vote.
- **Ch 3, Metal.** Strip the Steeple jobs, copper from the Copperdeep, and a town work, Give for the
  Bell, filled with gold and with things people bring. The pointless loss: the founder dies of a cough
  that went to the chest, and an apprentice has to finish. Reveal: the hungry at the Copperdeep are
  digging toward something old that they found rather than brought. Vote: cast a small bell now, or
  wait for Copperdeep metal and cast a big one (more jobs, more time for the King).
- **Ch 4, The Casting.** One night, the furnace lit in the square, Hold Till Morning inside the walls.
  The Tally Market can sit here or after, and his offer has an edge now: metal, or people.
- **Ch 5, The Hungry King.** "Ring every bell" means the town's bell plus every handbell and pot lid; a
  small bell means the bells spare fewer.

**Reveals:** ch 2 the tunnel under the pit; ch 3 the old thing below the Copperdeep; ch 5 why a bell
works, shown in play and never explained.
**Cost:** people's things (a ring each, the families' pots), the founder, the square dug up for a
season.
**Votes:** sewers, small or great bell, market, bells.

### Arc D: The Kept

**Pitch.** The enemy inside the walls and inside the heroes. The keep, the chapel and the Want are the
bible's three inward secrets, walked through slowly: the worst blow from your own side, and the thing
you carry hunting you. The darkest arc, and it depends on whether heroes turn.

- **Ch 2, The Light in the Window.** The Old Quarter is cleared; the Lane at Night. A figure in a good
  coat bows to the watch from the keep wall. Reveal: it answers to Lord Wick's name. The sewers run
  alongside or follow.
- **Ch 3, The Barred Keep.** Bible vote.
- **Ch 4, The Chapel [NEW].** If the Unmaking was used, its price is paid and the town learns the church
  kept a cure nailed shut while families lost people. If Wick was killed, the heirloom sits in
  Wickhold's hands and starts on whoever holds it. Vote: the church keeps the Lantern, or the council
  moves it to the town hall, and Odric's answer is the reveal's edge.
- **Ch 5, One of Ours.** If heroes turn, the first to turn is the chapter's butcher, with their class's
  moves, hunted by their old company (Settle a Name). If not, a townsperson who kept too much.
- **Ch 6, The Hungry King.** The final stack: the Lantern, what it eats, why it never went out.

**Reveals:** ch 2 Wick; ch 3 the Unmaking; ch 4 the church knew; ch 6 the Lantern.
**Cost:** the church's standing, a hero, whoever pays for the Unmaking.
**Votes:** the keep, the Lantern's custody, the bells.

### A suggested blend

Arc A's chapter order, Arc C's bell as the town works thread underneath it (the thing everyone gives
to), and Arc B's Count and Read the Wall as the daily jobs. Arc D's chapel chapter slots in after the
keep only if the town opens it.

## 3. Mission types

Already built: places (village, farm, field, forest, mine, the sewers), objectives (rescue, hunt,
larder, burn, story), the night twist, high and low roads, ledges and archers, cages and larder racks,
dens and oil with fire that spreads wood to wood, props the Warrior lifts and throws, chests and
chained chests, named butchers, deep jobs, goblins resting at their fires until they see you. Each
type below fits three to five rooms.

**1. The Count** (rescue, reworked)
- *"Lowfurrow, nine in the pens by the tithe barn at last count, and one of them's been in since
  harvest. Bring them all. Agne wants to see that one first."*
- Do: break the cages and walk everyone out; one captive is slow and not keen to come.
- Dread: humming in the last room, the tune slightly wrong; the gate shuts behind you.
- Twist: the slow one has the eyes, and what happens next is the town's problem.
- Feeds B, C. Built: rescue, cages, low road. Needs: captives who follow, a long-kept state.

**2. Settle a Name** (hunt, reworked)
- *"There's a new name on the hungry's side of the board, and Halvard says he sold it its first
  sword."*
- Do: hunt a butcher that was a hero, fighting with that class's moves.
- Dread: it calls you by name from the dark, in the voice you used to hire.
- Twist: it drops what it hoarded, and the church wants all of it.
- Feeds D. Built: hunt, class kits, named butchers. Needs: turning (Kyle's call), a butcher built on a
  kit.

**3. Strip the Steeple [NEW]**
- *"Tolmouth had three bells before it was a larder, and nobody's heard them since, which is either
  very good or very bad. Bring one home. It's heavier than you think, so bring somebody big."*
- Do: get a bell down and carry it out; one carrier crawls, two walk.
- Dread: the steeple creaks above you all the way in, and something up there shifts its weight.
- Twist: drop it and it rings, and everything near goes quiet and wrong for one breath, which is the
  only breath you'll get.
- Feeds C. Built: lift and throw, village kind. Needs: a bell any class can carry, a chapel block, a
  short ring stun.

**4. Carry It Home [NEW]**
- *"Ivo says there's a chained chest at the bottom of the Copperdeep that hums if you put your ear to
  it, and he'd like it up whole. Whoever carries it, the rest of you walk close."*
- Do: haul something the hungry want back up through the run.
- Dread: the rooms you cleared fill up behind you, and you can hear them following.
- Twist: on a long run it starts whispering prices.
- Feeds A, C. Built: chained chest, deep jobs, mine kind. Needs: a carry state, foes that go for the
  carrier.

**5. Bring Down the Workings [NEW]**
- *"They've dug a gallery under the east lane and propped it with our own fence posts. Knock the props
  out, get clear before the roof comes in, and count heads on the way up."*
- Do: break the pit props in order, then run.
- Dread: digging under your feet before you see a single goblin; then dust, and the way back is rock.
- Twist: the last gallery is dug toward the square.
- Feeds A, C. Built: mine kind, pit props, floor telegraphs. Needs: breakable props, a collapse that
  chases the party.

**6. Read the Wall [NEW]**
- *"There's a wall at Fennick Ford with more marks on it than Fennick Ford had people. Odric wants them
  copied, every five, and he'd rather a short count than nobody back."*
- Do: sneak in and hold still at the wall while the others keep watch.
- Dread: fires all round and nobody awake yet; one horn and every room wakes.
- Twist: the newest marks are fresh.
- Feeds B. Built: resting goblins, sneaking. Needs: hold to copy, a horn that wakes the whole run.

**7. Hold Till Morning** (planned as hold the ward)
- *"Nobody's on the camp fence tonight, and the horns started at dusk. Hold it till it's light. Hollis
  pays for every tent still standing."*
- Do: keep the hungry off a place for a set time.
- Dread: horns first, then torches on the far ridge, then counting them.
- Twist: tents burn, and the people in them run the wrong way.
- Feeds A, C. Built: burning wood, archers. Needs: waves and a timer (slice 5), tents.

**8. The Lane at Night [NEW]**
- *"Somebody's walking the keep wall in a good coat after midnight, and Pike swears it bowed to him. Go
  and look. Take the watch's lamp, theirs is brighter."*
- Do: follow a figure through the Old Quarter without losing it or starting a fight.
- Dread: it's always one corner ahead; the lane gate locks behind you.
- Twist: it isn't alone in there.
- Feeds A, D. Built: the town map, locked places. Needs: an Old Quarter run map, a figure that leads
  and won't fight.

**9. The Terms** (chapter 4 only)
- *"The Tally Man sent terms, in a dead man's hand. As many come out as went in, counted at the door.
  Bring our people, and decide on the way who stays."*
- Do: walk the market under its rules and bring captives out.
- Dread: you're counted in by name; everything watches and nothing attacks.
- Twist: every captive brought out means someone left behind, a hero or one of the town's escorts.
- Feeds A, B. Built: Fennick Ford, cages. Needs: a count at the door, escorts who can stay, the market
  map, the Tally Man.

**10. The Cart Road [NEW]** (posted by chapter 1's vote)
- Farm: *"The last barley wagon comes in from the north fields at dusk with Jos on the reins and nobody
  else. Walk beside it. If it's slow, don't tell Jos, he knows."*
- Refugees: *"Eleven from the camp want to walk in before the frost with everything they own on two
  handcarts. Walk with them, and carry what the old ones can't."*
- Do: escort through three rooms.
- Dread: the road runs through the wood at dusk, and something keeps pace in the trees.
- Twist: the cart stops in the second room and won't go on until the room is clear.
- Feeds A. Built: forest and field kinds, world flags. Needs: escort (slice 5), a cart on a path.

## 4. Fan theories as design fuel

Readers chew on what the books plant and don't pay off. Each row is Relic's own mystery in the same
spirit, nothing borrowed.

| Readers ask | Relic's own mystery | Planted | Paid off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where did the monsters really come from? | "Something was down there first" at the Copperdeep | Rumour, in the game now | Arc C ch 3 or the final chapter, never fully explained |
| Who really engineered the war? | Someone is calling them in: Barrowby emptied too fast, the Greyhollow field was left half-picked | Ch 1 and the prologue (done) | Ch 2 shows the digging they were called to; the stack names the King |
| Is any of the enemy decent? | No. But is anyone still in there? | Hungry voices copying the dead; the keep answering to Wick's name | The keep vote and the Unmaking |
| A host who claims kinship, then denies it | Whose light is in the keep's high window? | Town detail, in the bible | Ch 3 |
| Are the gods real and close? | Is the Lantern holy? | It never goes out; Odric changes the subject; censers go cold away from it | The final stack |
| Threads left dangling too long | What became of the eleven Hew burned? | The Stoker counting them as its meat (done) | The ledger's last page, Arc B **[NEW]** |
| Will people lose the next war? | Does Wickhold see the spring after chapter 5? | Rowan's songs, half wrong on purpose | The bells vote, never promised |

From the research: leave a couple open on purpose (where the hungry came from, what's under the
Copperdeep), and close at least one older one each chapter so players trust the story to pay out.

## 5. Risks

**Readers' complaints, and the guards.**
- **Drift.** Every job moves a chapter bar (people home, dens burned, metal given, marks copied), and
  no chapter leaves the valley. Arc B's extra chapters are the risk; if they sag, fold The Wall into
  What Lies Under.
- **Bonds formed too fast.** No arc has a romance. Any bond gets several jobs before it's spent (the
  founder over a chapter, Jos over the cart roads).
- **Rescue by magic.** Every save uses a rule planted at least a chapter earlier: bells (chapter 1),
  the nailed chapel and its chalked prayers (in town now), the Copperdeep (rumours now). Nothing new
  turns up at a vote to fix things.
- **Humour gone in the darkest stretch.** Arcs B and D are the dark ones. The town keeps its jokes (the
  watch and their boots, Moll's dice, Rowan getting the news wrong), and where jokes won't fit, named
  friends and Tollan's dog carry it **[NEW]**: the dog as a recurring town animal that never dies.
- **An enemy too strong to bother fighting.** The Hungry King needs the Lantern and can't take the
  walls; show him failing at things before chapter 5.
- **Telling the outcome early.** Rowan and the board foreshadow costs, never which way a vote goes.

**Overlaps the research flagged.**
- **Charcoal burners.** Hew stays the prologue's mentor death. No job avenges a burners' camp; the
  Stoker is one-off and stays dead. Arc B's ledger names Hew's eleven only as an entry.
- **A bell rung to call the hungry.** Never, on a traitor or as a trap. Bells hold and repel; the
  dropped bell in Strip the Steeple only stuns.
- **Captives turned into troops.** Turning is the Want working from inside, one person at a time. No
  bred, drugged or giant captives used as rams, including in chapter 5's horde.
- **Truce markets.** The Tally Market is one butcher's market and one appointment: no truce quarter, no
  neighbours trading, no sport. The Terms is a count at a door, never a contest or a wager, and the
  Arc A gate loss is a town law with no goblin at the line.
- **Creatures in tattoos.** Nothing goes into a hero's skin. Relics ride in the pack, the Want shows in
  the eyes, and Arc D's heirloom works on its bearer from the hand.
- **One more:** buying a map of the tunnels would echo the first book's sewer map. The refugee trader
  knows where the diggers were taken, not the way in.
