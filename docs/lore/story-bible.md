# Relic: the story bible

For the team and the lore keeper, never for players. This is what is true behind the scenes, where
the war is going, and what each chapter is really about. Players learn it a scrap at a time, from
what people say, what they find, and what the town decides. Read `voice.md` for how it's told.

## What everyone knows

A crown was made to want things. It wanted everything. When it broke, its shards went into the dark
places of the world, and in the dark, the shards made goblins: things that are nothing but wanting.
They're clever, they're cruel, they eat people, and they'd sell their own mothers for a better knife
(they have, and they've eaten the proceeds). Thirty years ago they came out of the hills in numbers
nobody had counted, and the kingdom lost. Not "suffered setbacks". Lost.

Wickhold is what's left: one walled town, a few thousand souls, a lantern in the square that
has never gone out, and a company of hired swords and odd talents who go out through the north gate
to bring people home. That's the game.

## What only we know

1. **The Lantern is a shard.** The Last Lantern burns on a splinter of the same crown. It isn't
   holy; it's *given up*. Every coin given to it, every vow sworn, every bit of want a person lets go
   of feeds it, and a shard that is fed what people give up turns that hunger into light. Father
   Odric knows this. Mother Isolde suspects. The goblins can smell it, and it's the reason they have
   never quite stopped circling Wickhold.
2. **The Want is in everyone.** Heroes who hoard (gold, loot, kills, glory) grow wanting. It's the
   shard-stuff in all of us. Give it away and you stay human. Keep it and, one day, you stop. The
   church's donations and vows are not decoration; they are the only medicine there is.
3. **Butchers carry slivers.** A goblin grows into a butcher by eating a sliver of shard (or a person
   who carried one). Named butchers are the ones who have eaten the most. What heroes pry out of them
   are relics: the same stuff as the Lantern, before anyone gave it up.
4. **Someone is gathering the pieces.** Not all goblins are content to raid. One of them, the
   Hungry King, is buying, stealing and eating his way toward enough shards to make the crown again.
   He doesn't need to take Wickhold. He needs the Lantern.
5. **The keep is not empty.** When the siege broke, Lord Aldric Wick barred himself in the keep with
   his household and the family's heirloom relic. He kept it. He kept everything. Nobody has come out
   in eleven years, and the thing in there still answers to his name.
6. **The chapel holds the answer, and it costs.** The barred Old Chapel holds the Unmaking: the relic
   the old church used to purify the shard-touched (and, rarely, to turn a goblin back into whoever it
   was eaten from). Using it costs the user something they love. Odric nailed the door shut himself.
7. **Bells.** Goblins hate bells. Not for magic; because a bell is a whole town agreeing on
   something at once, and nothing that is pure want can stand to hear that. Barrowby's bell in
   chapter 1 is the first hint the players get. It will matter.

## Where it's headed (the chapters)

Each chapter is a season of play: goals the whole world works toward, a story mission, and a council
vote at the end. The vote changes the town for good and decides which way the next chapter leans.
The order below is the spine; the votes bend it.

**1. The Last Lantern** (built). Bring people home; ring Barrowby's bell; decide the north fields
(farm them, or take in the refugees). *Under it:* the goblins pull back from Barrowby faster than
they should. Someone is calling them in.

**2. What Lies Under.** Burned dens on jobs drive the goblins below Wickhold to bay; the sewers open.
Down there: tunnels pointed at the square, at the Lantern. Sergeant Dunn's watch has been bribed (in
gold, and worse: in promises). *Vote:* seal the sewers (safe, the town shrinks into itself) or go down
after whoever dug them (dangerous, and the Hungry King learns our names).

**3. The Barred Keep.** The Old Quarter is cleared (town works), the keep's gate cracks, and something
in noble rags walks the walls at night. The heroes go in. Lord Wick is most of the way to goblin.
*Vote:* kill him and take the heirloom relic (strength), or open the chapel and use the Unmaking on him
(he might come back; someone pays the price; the town learns the church's secret).

**4. The Tally Market.** A goblin market in the old mill town of Fennick Ford, where people are coin.
The Tally Man (a butcher who keeps accounts) offers Wickhold a trade: our people back, for something
of ours. *Vote:* trade (people come home; the goblins get what they wanted, and it's a piece of the
crown), or burn the market (everyone in it dies, theirs and ours, and the Tally Man's ledger is lost:
the only record of where every captive went).

**5. The Hungry King.** He comes for the Lantern with everything he has. The choice the whole story
has been building to: carry the Lantern out into the dark to break him (the town goes dark, maybe
forever), or ring every bell in Wickhold at once and hold (the town as one voice, and whoever has the
most Want among the heroes is the one the bells don't spare).

## How players shape it

- **Town works** are the town choosing with its gold and its feet: what gets cleared, built and opened
  sets which chapter's door opens first.
- **Votes** are chapter endings. Nothing is decided by the writers that the council can decide.
- **Feats** are read at the weekly review: the hero who saved the most at Barrowby gets named in the
  next chapter's opening; a hero who hoarded and turned becomes the next named butcher, with their
  own name, their class's moves, and their old friends hunting them.
- **Nobody is safe**: townsfolk the players love can die between chapters if the town chose badly.
  That's what makes the choice a choice.

## Things that never change

- Goblins are never misunderstood. They are clever, funny in a horrible way, and evil. The one mercy
  in this world is the Unmaking, and it's rare, costly and late.
- The church is not wrong, but it is not honest either.
- Wickhold is decent people being petty, brave, frightened and kind on the same afternoon.
- Gold is never just gold. Every coin is either kept or given, and the world notices which.
