# Relic: the story bible

For the team and the lore keeper, never for players. This is what is true behind the scenes, where
the war is going, and what each chapter is really about. Players learn it a scrap at a time, from
what people say, what they find, and what the town decides. Read `voice.md` for how it's told.

## What everyone knows

A crown was made to want things. It wanted everything. When it broke, its shards went into the dark
places of the world, and in the dark, the shards made goblins: things that are nothing but wanting.
They're clever, they're cruel, they eat people, and they'd sell their own mothers for a better knife
(they have, and they've eaten the proceeds). Thirty years ago they came out of the hills in numbers
nobody had counted, and the kingdom lost. Not "suffered setbacks". Lost.

Wickhold is what's left: one walled town, a few thousand souls, a lantern in the square that
has never gone out, and a company of hired swords and odd talents who go out through the north gate
to bring people home. That's the game.

## What only we know

1. **The Lantern is a shard.** The Last Lantern burns on a splinter of the same crown. It isn't
   holy; it's *given up*. Every coin given to it, every vow sworn, every bit of want a person lets go
   of feeds it, and a shard that is fed what people give up turns that hunger into light. Father
   Odric knows this. Mother Isolde suspects. The goblins can smell it, and it's the reason they have
   never quite stopped circling Wickhold.
2. **The Want is in everyone.** Heroes who hoard (gold, loot, kills, glory) grow wanting. It's the
   shard-stuff in all of us. Give it away and you stay human. Keep it and, one day, you stop. The
   church's donations and vows are not decoration; they are the only medicine there is.
3. **Butchers carry slivers.** A goblin grows into a butcher by eating a sliver of shard (or a person
   who carried one). Named butchers are the ones who have eaten the most. What heroes pry out of them
   are relics: the same stuff as the Lantern, before anyone gave it up.
4. **Someone is gathering the pieces.** Not all goblins are content to raid. One of them, the
   Hungry King, is buying, stealing and eating his way toward enough shards to make the crown again.
   He doesn't need to take Wickhold. He needs the Lantern.
5. **The keep is not empty.** When the siege broke, Lord Aldric Wick barred himself in the keep with
   his household and the family's heirloom relic. He kept it. He kept everything. Nobody has come out
   in eleven years, and the thing in there still answers to his name.
6. **The chapel holds the answer, and it costs.** The barred Old Chapel holds the Unmaking: the relic
   the old church used to purify the shard-touched (and, rarely, to turn a goblin back into whoever it
   was eaten from). Using it costs the user something they love. Odric nailed the door shut himself.
7. **Bells.** Goblins hate bells. Not for magic; because a bell is a whole town agreeing on
   something at once, and nothing that is pure want can stand to hear that. Barrowby's bell in
   chapter 1 is the first hint the players get. It will matter.

## Where it's headed (the chapters)

Each chapter is a season of play: goals the whole world works toward, a story mission, and a council
vote at the end. The vote changes the town for good and decides which way the next chapter leans.
The order below is the spine; the votes bend it.

**1. The Last Lantern** (built). Bring people home; ring Barrowby's bell; decide the north fields
(farm them, or take in the refugees). *Under it:* the goblins pull back from Barrowby faster than
they should. Someone is calling them in.

**2. What Lies Under.** Burned dens on jobs drive the goblins below Wickhold to bay; the sewers open.
Down there: tunnels pointed at the square, at the Lantern. Sergeant Dunn's watch has been bribed (in
gold, and worse: in promises). *Vote:* seal the sewers (safe, the town shrinks into itself) or go down
after whoever dug them (dangerous, and the Hungry King learns our names).

**3. The Barred Keep.** The Old Quarter is cleared (town works), the keep's gate cracks, and something
in noble rags walks the walls at night. The heroes go in. Lord Wick is most of the way to goblin.
*Vote:* kill him and take the heirloom relic (strength), or open the chapel and use the Unmaking on him
(he might come back; someone pays the price; the town learns the church's secret).

**4. The Tally Market.** A goblin market in the old mill town of Fennick Ford, where people are coin.
The Tally Man (a butcher who keeps accounts) offers Wickhold a trade: our people back, for something
of ours. *Vote:* trade (people come home; the goblins get what they wanted, and it's a piece of the
crown), or burn the market (everyone in it dies, theirs and ours, and the Tally Man's ledger is lost:
the only record of where every captive went).

**5. The Hungry King.** He comes for the Lantern with everything he has. The choice the whole story
has been building to: carry the Lantern out into the dark to break him (the town goes dark, maybe
forever), or ring every bell in Wickhold at once and hold (the town as one voice, and whoever has the
most Want among the heroes is the one the bells don't spare).

## How players shape it

- **Town works** are the town choosing with its gold and its feet: what gets cleared, built and opened
  sets which chapter's door opens first.
- **Votes** are chapter endings. Nothing is decided by the writers that the council can decide.
- **Feats** are read at the weekly review: the hero who saved the most at Barrowby gets named in the
  next chapter's opening; a hero who hoarded and turned becomes the next named butcher, with their
  own name, their class's moves, and their old friends hunting them.
- **Nobody is safe**: townsfolk the players love can die between chapters if the town chose badly.
  That's what makes the choice a choice.

## Things that never change

- Goblins are never misunderstood. They are clever, funny in a horrible way, and evil. The one mercy
  in this world is the Unmaking, and it's rare, costly and late.
- The church is not wrong, but it is not honest either.
- Wickhold is decent people being petty, brave, frightened and kind on the same afternoon.
- Gold is never just gold. Every coin is either kept or given, and the world notices which.

## Recorded lore (from the lore keeper's first review)

- **Timeline.** The war began thirty years ago. Wickhold's siege lasted three winters and broke eleven
  years ago. Old Wenna (seventy-one) saw the walls go up.
- **Where new goblins come from.** Not breeding. Captives kept in the pens and fed on what the butchers
  leave turn, slowly, over a season: the Want takes them from the inside. The pens are for that as
  much as for the larder, which is why every rescue matters twice.
- **Habits.** The hungry hoard lamp oil, the way they hoard everything: more than they can use. It's
  greed, not stupidity, and it burns.
- **The valley.** Barrowfields (Barrowby, Tollan's Farm, Lowfurrow Fields), Greyhollow (the Charcoal
  Camps, Hangman's Wood), Copperdeep (the Copperdeep Shafts: they took the ones who could dig, which
  seeds chapter 2's tunnels), Fenmere (Fennick Ford, the tally wall that seeds chapter 4). Tolmouth was a
  town and is now a larder; Dessa's steel came from there. "Something was down there first" at the
  Copperdeep: an old shard, deep, that the goblins found rather than brought. Keep it for later.
- **No dodge for Warriors and Healers** (recorded 2026-11-01, Kyle's call): they block, and walk out of
  anything with a striped edge; the wood teaches them to STEP OUT. Every other class dodges its own way:
  the Rogue dashes, the Ranger jumps, the Mage blinks.
- **Class skills** (recorded 2026-11-01, Kyle's call): a hero comes out of the Greyhollow wood with
  their weapon, their own way out of a blow and the Relic Javelin, and knows no class skill; nothing in the wood
  uses one, and no line says they've forgotten or lost it. Each class's own trainer teaches its skill
  from level 3 for 150 gold (it was level 2 for 60 until 2026-11-01): Master Cole the Shield Charge and the War Cry (both at once; the hero carries one, and changes it at his yard), Huntress Maeve the Hunter's Net, Whisper
  Shadowstep, Magister Vell the Elemental Channel (Vell taught the Channel: settled), Mother Isolde
  Sanctuary. From level 5, for 250 gold (was level 3 for 80), Maeve teaches the snare on the jump and Vell the image on the
  blink (the Mage's own craft through the focus, not shard-stuff; the goblins go for it a couple of
  seconds and no longer). Paying is trade and never touches the Want. Until Vell teaches the Channel a
  Mage's spells are fire. Old heroes keep what they had; no in-world explanation is ever written.
- **Trainers' fees** (recorded 2026-11-01): drills are bought from your own class's trainer, and
  paying for them is trade, not keeping or giving: it never touches the Want. Mother Isolde's fees buy
  linen and Sister's Bitter for the infirmary, never the Lantern box. Magister Vell teaches the Elemental
  Channel and sells the FIRST SPELL drill (was QUICK CHANNEL until the Channel became a quick switch, 2026-11-02). Players see "breath", never
  "stamina" or "wind". A job's length in real minutes is the game's own voice on the board only;
  characters and postings never measure a job in minutes.
- **The valley, added** (recorded 2026-11-01, for the river, marsh and ruins jobs): Barrowfields: the
  Sheepwash (the shallow stretch where the valley washed its sheep, deep either side), Ketterby (a hamlet
  by Barrowby, burned in the war's first year, never rebuilt). Greyhollow: Sallow Carr (a willow wood
  gone to bog when nobody kept the ditches). Copperdeep: Brokenbridge (the ore road's crossing, bridge
  down; who broke it is open). Fenmere: the Peat Cuts (old diggings gone to water), Hesketh Hall (a
  family's house on the one dry rise in the fen, razed; no Heskeths left). Hesketh Hall is not a second
  Wick: no heirloom, no barred lord, the statues are just statues.
- **Named butchers on the board:** the Gnawer (Barrowby), Mawl, Skarn, Old Knuckles, Old Spoon,
  Gutchain, Mother Rust. The Tally Man is chapter 4's and never appears on the board before then.
- **People.** Corporal Brisk and Squire Pell (barracks), Farmer Jos, Old Merren (the Old Quarter),
  Alder Bram ("Alder" is a council seat) and Goodwife Sell, Dessa, Pike and Orla of the watch, the
  eight Rescue Row families, the patients Amos, Letty and Corin. Whisper is "he".
- **Town details.** Some nights there's a light in the keep's high window. Prayers are chalked over the
  chapel planks. The well is as old as the town and has tasted of copper since the siege.
- **Words players see.** "Deep job" for the daily long job with a named butcher at the bottom (never
  "deep dive"). Stamina is "breath". Heroes are a company; you "hire" another.
- **Halvard** oils every blade on his rack, and the next one before you've paid for this one.
- **The censer coal** (Kyle, 2026-11-01: kept). Every Healer's coal is lit from the Last Lantern when
  the church takes them on: the Lantern's light, not a piece of the shard, so the Lantern loses nothing.
  A dead butcher leaves a crumb of the sliver it ate (apart from any relic pried out of it); out of a
  body and unfed it goes dark in moments. Fed to the coal, its hunger burns into light the Healer
  spends on other people. Healing is giving, so it doesn't grow the Want in the Healer. But the coal
  keeps a little of every crumb: to the hungry it smells more like the Lantern each time, and they
  can't tell kept from given. A full censer is the nearest thing to the Lantern outside the walls.
- **On screen:** "coals" and "the censer", never shard, sliver, crown or the Want, and never why the
  hungry come for a Healer, only that they do. Isolde suspects ("I don't ask the coal its business");
  Odric knows and changes the subject.
- **Open question for Kyle (chapter 5):** do censer coals count toward the Want when the bells "don't
  spare" the one with the most? Recorded answer for now: no, because they're spent on others. Darker
  option: they count for a Healer who carries a full censer and rarely heals.
- **Loot words** (lore keeper, 2026-11-01): tiers Plain, Fine, Forged, Tolmouth (pre-war steel from a
  town that's now a larder; nobody makes it now), Hungry (the town's word for goblins). Hungry gear is
  plain steel that sat in a butcher's fist beside a sliver until the hunger took: the same stuff as a
  relic, a different kind of thing. "Steel", not scrap, is what melting gives (scrappers are goblins).
  Sister's Bitter (Agne makes it), Wall Brandy (the watch drinks it), Lamp Oil (the hungry hoard it).
- **The Want, as built:** a hidden number per hero; grows with hungry gear worn and used, falls with
  gold given at the Lantern and hungry gear given up at the church (which feeds the Lantern). Its tell
  is the eyes. Turning into a named butcher is still Kyle's call.
- **On screen, "hungry gear"** is the term for butcher-touched gear (red). It can only be given up at the
  church, never melted, traded or dropped.
- **The prologue** (2026-11-01, Kyle approved by building it): every new hero wakes on the field where the
  Greyhollow column died (undated: every hero plays it, whatever chapter the world is in). Hew, the last
  charcoal burner in Greyhollow, burned the column's dead so the hungry went without; he gave the hero
  his boots, taught them to fight, and was killed by the Stoker, a one-off butcher with a burner's rake.
  He left a hazel tally stick (eleven notches, names by the ones he knew) for Father Odric, who keeps the
  count of the dead. The hungry left that field half-picked and went north together (unexplained).
  Wickhold owes Hew for four loads of charcoal. Orla of the watch knew him by his boots. The boots were resoled twice and let the wet in at the left heel; he gives them to the hero, who reaches Wickhold with that foot wet through. He had been round every one of the field's dead since first light before he found the hero alive. He carries cordwood up the hill to his hut. The Stoker counts the eleven Hew burned as its own meat lost ("Eleven you burn. Eleven of mine. So, this one I keep."); whether "this one" is the hero or Hew is left open on screen. Hew sizes up each class when he finds them: a Warrior may have been with the column (he leaves it unasked); a Healer's censer is lit at the Lantern, not off a kiln; he keeps a hand on his purse around a Rogue and assumes they've had practice; a Ranger's bowstring got a soaking; he doesn't hold with magic (a backwoods view, not the town's: Wickhold has Vell's tower) and a Mage's focus is still humming. The hero's gender is never stated in the prologue; Hew calls them "stray". Hew's death, as built: round the back of his hut by the cordwood, the Stoker comes up from behind the stack, runs him down, lifts him and bites his head off, then throws the body up onto its cordwood pile like a log (kept, not wasted), turns on the hero and bellows its line. No spoken or written line ever mentions the head. The Stoker's relic is the Relic Javelin, grown in under its breastbone, levered out with Hew's poker; every hero's first relic. The grappling hook is the next one found. The hero walks west out of the Greyhollow wood to Wickhold (Greyhollow lies east of the town; the hungry from the field went north). Open: does anyone ever burn Hew?
- **After the wood:** the tally stick has eleven notches; Odric cuts the twelfth, for Hew, when it's handed
  over. Odric knew Hew and owed him a beer he never came in for. Hew calls the hero "stray". A Healer's
  censer goes cold on the field ("They do"); only the church relights it, and Isolde carries it there herself.

- **The Ranger's hound** (recorded 2026-11-01; changed by Kyle 2026-11-01): a rough grey wolfhound bred at
  Huntress Maeve's lodge and sold out of it as a pup, and the Ranger's own dog before the column ever set out. It was
  on the Greyhollow column with them. When the column broke it lay across their legs all night while they were out,
  and made no sound with the hungry feeding on the dead a few yards off. The hungry left it alone because they were
  busy at the dead, not because they fear dogs. It is at the Ranger's heel from the waking on, through the wood and
  against the Stoker. At the lodge, Maeve knows her own breeding on sight and has opinions about how it's been fed.
  An ordinary dog, no shard and no magic. On screen it never dies: it bolts when a butcher swings close, the Stoker
  included, and comes back when it's ready. Nothing ever jokes about a dead dog. No name on screen yet, and no he or
  she yet. Dogs are not a goblin weakness; bells stay the town's one hope.

- **Hew at the waking** (recorded 2026-11-01): he comes in low out of the trees west of the hero and round the
  back of the goblins feeding on the dead, keeping to the far side of the field from them, and stays crouched until
  he hauls the hero up. The feeders came back to the field after his round at first light.

- **Elements** (recorded 2026-11-01): fire, frost and storm are the world's only three, shared by a Mage's spells and
  the relic-wright's fittings. A Mage's Elemental Channel is its own craft, worked through its focus (the wand, staff
  or rod): not shard-stuff, and it doesn't feed the Want. The relic-wright's fittings are for skills and relics; a
  Mage's spells take their element from the Channel. Settled 2026-11-01: Magister Vell teaches it.

## The breach and the mending (recorded 2026-11-01, Kyle's call; lore keeper)
- A recent assault, never dated on screen, brought down the north wall on either side of the north gate. The gate held; the hungry came over the wall. The rest is the old stone Wenna saw go up, so Wickhold is still the last walled town. The siege (three winters, broke eleven years ago) is older and separate.
- The dead of the breach were carried on Odric's cart and buried by the church. Odric said the words over each, by name where he knew it.
- Raids: the hungry come over the north wall at night, take nobody, and pull down what the town has mended. Stone holds. Nobody on screen says why (never the Lantern by name).
- The hungry whistle going over a wall, and something past the wall whistles back at the watch, so the watch doesn't whistle on duty. Relic's own.
- Widow Perrin keeps the physic garden west of the well, her mother's before her (sage, feverfew, roses). A dead apple tree she won't cut; why stays unsaid.
- The Fosters live in the lanes (the dresser). Gannet's tavern sign is crooked and he likes it. Hob's mother is alive.
- Nothing comes into Wickhold from outside: seed, glass and oil are dear because nobody brings any in.
- Cobb is the church's gravedigger; he takes the breach dead off Odric's cart. The Tulls lived against the north wall and were burned out (nobody died); stone from their house went into the mending. Mab draws water at the well. Wat is a recruit at the barracks under Master Cole, with Corporal Brisk and Squire Pell.
- An empty gallows at the square's edge, a crow on the beam; nobody on screen says who it was last used for. The crow is just a crow.
- Rapiers and hand crossbows are pre-war pieces or Halvard's work from melted steel; none is ever said to have come from outside (recorded 2026-11-01).

## Dens and big houses (recorded 2026-11-01; Kyle's call, lore keeper)
- Big valley houses: the longhouse, the hall house and the great barn. Several can stand in one place; none has a named lord.
- The hungry eat raw where they kill and cook what they carry home; the den cookpot is for people. Larders hang their stock by the ankles from rafter hooks. Captives are caged beside what's left of the last one, never explained on screen.
- Den interiors are a deliberate exception to "Show, don't gore" (Kyle: "goblins are brutal, we need to showcase it"). The limit: no children's remains, ever (show the shoe, never the child).
- Why a larder burns: their hoarded lamp oil catches. A captive left in a burning house is lost; no line ever jokes about it.

## Spiders, the halls and names (recorded 2026-11-01; Kyle's call, lore keeper)
- Spiders: brood mothers grew fat in the Copperdeep shafts on what the hungry left there; nothing stranger than that. They come in waves and their bite poisons. Webs hung with what they caught follow the den limit (no children's remains, ever).
- The cellars under the Peat Cuts and Hesketh Hall hold larders.
- Vell's tower floor: a chalked circle cut into three by three lines, one part each for fire, frost and storm, with Vell's own sums chalked round the rim, rubbed out and redone. Never a star.
- Cleric is the on-screen name for the Healer class (Kyle's call). Older notes here that say Healer mean the Cleric.
- Element jobs (Kyle's call, 2026-11-02): fire for swarms, frost for the big and the quick, storm for packs and shields (lightning goes round a board). The Stoker lives by the kiln: fire hardly touches it and cold is what hurts. Butchers, named ones too, take more from frost and less from fire. Spiders and their nests burn easily. The brute being cold-blooded stays off screen.
- Dens on burn jobs are low, windowless huts the hungry put up themselves, not taken houses (recorded 2026-11-02). The longhouse, hall house and great barn stay the big houses.
